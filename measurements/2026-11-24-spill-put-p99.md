# Spill index Step 1 put-latency breakdown across 1, 4, 16, and 64 writers (#316)

Run [45360908586](https://github.com/iskandarnorazli/Awanfs/actions/runs/45360908586) on GitHub-hosted 4-vCPU runners (`ubuntu-latest` job `138330451320`, `windows-latest` job `138330451013`), comparing `redb` (`redb_spill::RedbSpill`) and `prod` (`s3_server::spill::SpillIndex`, 64 MiB spill cache budget and 16 MiB memtable).

## Verdict

Step 1 disproves the premise in `#316` that flushes and compactions under `state.write()` set the 64-writer put P99, and triggers `#316`'s Stop condition:

1. **Flushes and compactions do not set 64-writer P99.** Across every `writers` run (`1`, `4`, `16`, `64`), `prod` ran `flush_count = 1` flush (`44.191-47.415 ms` on Linux, `100.852-113.410 ms` on Windows) and `compaction_count = 0` compactions (`compact_time_ms = 0.002-0.010 ms`). Excluding the single flushing put (`non_flush_p99_us`) leaves 64-writer P99 virtually unchanged: `799.94 -> 799.87 us` on Linux and `1,246.00 -> 1,245.70 us` on Windows.
2. **Lock wait dominates 99.6%-99.8% of put P99.** At 64 writers, `lock_hold_p99_us` is only `2.916 us` on Linux (`P50 0.631 us`) and `2.400 us` on Windows (`P50 1.000 us`), while `lock_wait_p99_us` is `798.268 us` on Linux and `1,240.900 us` on Windows.
3. **4 writers vs 64 writers and `redb` tail latency.** At `4` writers (1 writer per vCPU), `prod` put P99 is `35.30 us` on Linux and `41.00 us` on Windows (`3.2x` and `5.0x` below `redb`'s `111.81 us` and `203.10 us`). At `16` and `64` writers on 4 vCPUs, contention on the single `parking_lot::RwLock<State>` parks writers in the kernel, and waking them across 16-64 runnable threads adds 4-vCPU OS scheduler wait (`182.973 -> 798.268 us` Linux; `359.300 -> 1,240.900 us` Windows). `redb` reports lower P99 (`127.59 us` Linux, `226.60 us` Windows) only because its `Mutex` allows hot threads to barge while starving parked threads, pushing `redb` P99.9 to `2,447,424.03 us` (`2.45 s`) on Linux and `4,389,068.40 us` (`4.39 s`) on Windows against `prod`'s `1,391.77 us` and `2,088.00 us`.
4. **Stop condition hit.** Eliminating flush waits (`44-113 ms` per flush) while keeping `get` and `scan_prefix` consistent requires a second memtable and a background flush thread, both on `#316`'s Stop list. Eliminating single-`RwLock` contention across concurrent writers without starvation requires partitioning (`#322`, Decision 69) or memtable sharding (also a design change).
